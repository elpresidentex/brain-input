The app

Brain Input runs on your Mac. I do not operate any server for it and receive no data from it. There is no account, no analytics, no crash reporting and no telemetry.

  • Audio. Speech is recognised on your Mac. Audio is held in memory while it is transcribed and then discarded. It is not written to disk and not sent anywhere. This includes meeting recordings.
  • Notes. Dictation history and meeting notes are plain files in ~/BrainInput/. Settings, your dictionary and a log without dictated text are in your Library folder. Delete them whenever you like.
  • Speech model download. The setup guide downloads the model you choose from Hugging Face (Hugging Face, Inc., USA). Hugging Face sees your IP address for that download.
  • Clean-up with Claude (optional). Only if you enter your own Anthropic API key, the app sends text to the Claude API of Anthropic, PBC, USA: the transcript, the name of the app and window you are dictating into, your dictionary and style notes, and, in coding apps, text visible in that window. For meeting summaries it sends the meeting transcript. You are Anthropic's customer for this; their commercial terms and privacy policy apply. Remove the key in Settings to stop it.
  • API key. Stored in your macOS Keychain, sent only to Anthropic.
  • Calendar (optional). If you allow it, the app reads the current calendar event on your Mac to title a meeting note and list participants. Nothing is sent, except that these names become part of the note and, with a key, of the text summarised by Claude.
  • Updates. The app does not check for updates by itself. The link in Settings opens the GitHub releases page in your browser.

Recording other people

If you record a call, you are responsible for telling the other participants and, where the law requires it, getting their consent. In Germany, recording the non-public spoken word of others without consent is a criminal offence (§ 201 StGB). The app shows a reminder each time before a recording starts.
